5 Easy Dishes You Can Make with Gobi Manchurian Masala

One versatile blend, five restaurant-style dishes. Here's how to get the most out of a single pack of Gobi Manchurian Masala.

RS Chef'z Editorial Team18 September 2026 2 min read

A jar of single-use masala is a bit of a waste of shelf space. The blends worth keeping are the ones that pull double (or triple) duty, and RS Chef'z Gobi Manchurian Masala is built exactly that way. The ginger-garlic-chilli backbone that makes Gobi Manchurian sing works just as well on mushrooms, paneer and more.

Here are five easy dishes you can make with a single pack, from the obvious to the ones you might not have tried yet.

1. Classic Gobi Manchurian

The dish the blend is named for, and still the best place to start. Crisp-fried cauliflower florets tossed in a glossy, spicy-sweet sauce, a restaurant favourite that's very doable at home once you get the batter and frying right.

We've written the full method, with the crispiness tricks, in how to make restaurant-style Gobi Manchurian at home.

2. Mushroom Fry

Swap cauliflower for button mushrooms and the same blend gives you a quick, meaty-tasting fry. Halve the mushrooms, toss them with a spoon of masala, a little ginger-garlic and a pinch of cornflour, then sear on high heat until they're golden at the edges. Because mushrooms release water, keep the pan hot and don't crowd it, you want them fried, not stewed. Ready in under fifteen minutes.

Keep mushrooms crisp

Cook mushrooms on high heat in a wide pan and in a single layer. Crowding traps steam and turns them soft, space and heat keep the edges browned.

3. Paneer Tikka

The blend makes a fuss-free tikka marinade. Mix a spoon of masala into thick curd with a little oil, coat cubes of paneer along with onion and capsicum, and rest for half an hour. Thread onto skewers and cook on a grill, in the oven, or on a tawa until charred at the edges. It's a vegetarian starter that tastes like it came off a tandoor.

4. Crispy Baby Corn or Paneer 65

The Manchurian coating technique isn't fussy about what's inside it. Battered baby corn or paneer cubes, fried crisp and tossed with the masala, make an instant party starter. Use the same thick flour-and-cornflour batter from the Gobi recipe and fry in hot oil in small batches.

5. Manchurian Fried Rice or Noodles

Turn leftovers into a meal. A spoon of the masala stirred through fried rice or hakka noodles, with a splash of soy and some spring onion, carries the same Indo-Chinese flavour into a main course. Add any leftover Gobi Manchurian on top and you've got a full plate with almost no extra effort.

Why One Good Blend Beats a Shelf of Jars

Cooking with a single, well-made masala keeps things consistent: the flavour is the same every time, you're not measuring five spices per dish, and there's far less to store. RS Chef'z Gobi Manchurian Masala is made with no artificial colours, no preservatives and no artificial flavours, so what you taste is the spices, ground fresh.

Final Thoughts

The mark of a great masala is versatility. If a single pack can turn out Gobi Manchurian, a quick mushroom fry, paneer tikka and a fried-rice dinner, it's not a one-trick jar. It's a cupboard essential. Start with the classic Gobi Manchurian and work your way down the list.
