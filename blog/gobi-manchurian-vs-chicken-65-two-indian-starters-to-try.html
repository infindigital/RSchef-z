Gobi Manchurian vs Chicken 65: Two Popular Indian Starters to Try at Home

Two icons of the Indian starter menu, side by side, how Gobi Manchurian and Chicken 65 differ, and which to cook tonight.

RS Chef'z Editorial Team16 September 2026 2 min read

Scan almost any Indian restaurant's starter section and two names jump out: Gobi Manchurian and Chicken 65. Both are crisp, both are spicy, and both have a devoted following, but they come from different traditions and deliver quite different experiences. If you're deciding what to cook, here's how they stack up.

Where They Come From

Gobi Manchurian is Indo-Chinese, part of the inventive street-food cuisine that grew out of the Chinese community in Kolkata and spread nationwide. It pairs a Chinese-style sauce of soy, vinegar, ginger and garlic with a very Indian love of chilli and crunch.

Chicken 65 is South Indian, famously associated with Chennai. It's a deep-fried, heavily spiced chicken starter finished with curry leaves and green chilli, a dry, punchy dish with no sauce to speak of.

Flavour and Texture

The headline difference is the finish. Gobi Manchurian is saucy and glossy, the fried florets are tossed in a spicy-sweet, tangy sauce that clings to each piece. Chicken 65 is dry and fiery, the flavour lives in the marinade and the curry-leaf tempering, not in a sauce.

Texturally they share a goal, a crisp exterior, but Gobi keeps a tender vegetable centre while Chicken 65 gives you juicy meat inside a crackling crust. One is bright and sweet-sour; the other is hot, savoury and aromatic.

Effort and Timing

Both are weeknight-friendly, with a small difference:

  • Gobi Manchurian has two active stages: fry the florets, then make the sauce. So there's a little more juggling at the end.
  • Chicken 65 is mostly hands-off: marinate, fry, temper. The marinade does the work while you get on with other things.

Neither takes more than about 45 minutes start to finish, and both reward a well-judged fry more than any advanced skill.

Which Should You Make?

Cooking for a mixed or vegetarian table? Gobi Manchurian is the crowd-pleaser, nobody misses the meat. Want something bold, dry and beer-snack-shaped? Chicken 65 is your dish. Feeding a crowd? Make both, they're a classic pairing, and the contrast of saucy and dry keeps the table interesting.
